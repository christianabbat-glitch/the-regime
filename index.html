The Regime

Setting the table…

    Changes apply from the next step of this game, and to new games.

    You sit at the bottom of the table; the computer plays the others. All 50 names from your list are on the board every game. The starting Politburo is always the same old guard on black tiles, owned by nobody and never dealt: Ordzhonikidze (Industry), Mekhlis (Pravda), Voroshilov (Red Army), Yagoda (NKVD), Yezhov (Cadres) and Molotov at Stalin's right hand. The Judiciary is not part of the Politburo: its head, the Chairman of the Military Collegium, is dealt at random like everyone else. Kalinin is always Head of State, alone in the corner of the board on a white tile: a figurehead whom Stalin's cards never touch (players can denounce him and the NKVD can arrest him; once he is gone his seat stays empty). Everyone else is placed at random. The ranks are printed down the side of the board; Stalin's cards name the Politburo, a rank, a department or a single seat, and only the cards aimed at the Judiciary can touch the Chairman.

    The round. 1 Stalin's card: the top card of Stalin's deck is turned over and played, and nobody chooses it; the clock then moves the number of boxes printed on the card (0, 1 or 2). 2 Every player's turn: two actions, which must be different. 3 The NKVD fills its quota. 4 The trial. 5 The round ends. Fuse arrests are the exception: they happen the moment a man reaches the fuse.

    The clock has 15 boxes in four colours, one per stage; the marker starts in box 1. When a card moves it into a new colour, the round still runs under the old stage, and when the round ends the Plan is reviewed, a mass trial empties the Lubyanka, and the new stage begins. The round in which the marker reaches box 15 is the last: when it ends, the Terror simply stops. There is no final trial; men still in the Butyrka or the Lubyanka are left in limbo.

    Actions (two a turn, never the same one twice): Denounce (place a chip from your own supply on anyone), Clear (take a chip off anyone and add it to your supply), Move (swap any man with the seat touching his, above, below or beside; Stalin's right hand sits between the NKVD and Cadres heads and only those two can swap with him; an empty seat can be moved into), a department power, Flee, Special Tasks, Work for the Plan, or nothing. Only a man's real owner can use his power, and only by turning his card face up; he stays face up.

    Chips are ammunition. Every player starts with 3 chips in his supply, whatever the number of players. A denunciation spends one; with an empty supply you can't denounce. Clearing a chip off anyone, or Industry's Protect, puts the chips into your own supply. Stalin's cards, the chip that falls when a man is arrested, the chip a defendant throws at his trial and the chips the Chairman and the NKVD chief take as penalties all come from Stalin's bank, which never runs out; an arrested man's chips go back to it.

    Work for the Plan means taking the Challenge Red Banner, from the middle or from whoever holds it. Whoever holds the Banner when the stage changes awards the Order of Lenin to any man on the board, and keeps the Banner. No player can put a chip on a man wearing the Order of Lenin (no denunciation, no Rewrite, no trial chip); Stalin's cards, the NKVD and the chip that falls when the man above him is arrested still reach him. He wears it until he is arrested.

    Arrests. The moment a man is arrested, by the fuse or by the NKVD, suspicion falls on the man directly below him in his column, who takes a chip; if that pushes him to the fuse, he falls too, and the wave can run down the column. (Stalin's right hand and the Head of State have nobody below them.) The arrested man's other chips come off; he carries one chip to his trial. Empty seats then fill from below. Stalin's right hand stays empty until the NKVD or Cadres head moves into it.

    Stalin's right hand counts like any other seat at the end. When seats empty, the game asks who controls him: his owner may show his card and make the appointments, choosing for each empty seat which man below it is promoted (everyone between moves up), or stay hidden and let the men below move up as usual. Once shown, he appoints every time. The chip that fell on the man directly below stays where it fell.

    The Politburo, the five department heads above the dashed line and Stalin's right hand, is protected in one way: the NKVD can't arrest them for its quota. Anyone can still denounce them, or Move one of them out of the top rank, after which he is fair game. Nobody is protected from Stalin.

    The NKVD makes its quota of arrests every round, however many men the fuse has already taken, choosing among men who carry chips. Its arrests wait in the Butyrka, a prison van painted "Bread", whose shutter slides back as the stages change to open more places; at the end of the NKVD phase everyone in it moves on into the Lubyanka. Fuse arrests go straight to the Lubyanka. When the NKVD is called on, whoever controls the man in the NKVD chief's seat must show his card; if nobody does, he is arrested (counting toward the quota) and the next man moves up and is asked in turn. If the NKVD is emptied, its duty falls to the highest seat with a man in it.

    Trials. At the end of each round one prisoner stands trial: whoever has been held longest. At a stage change a mass trial tries everyone in the Lubyanka, in the order they were arrested. Before his verdict, each defendant's owner may show his card and place his one chip on anyone; if nobody speaks for him, he names nobody. Everyone on trial is guilty: the die decides death or the Gulag, death on a 1 in stage 1, on 1–2 in stage 2, on 1–3 in stage 3 and on 1–4 in the final stage. The Chairman of the Military Collegium may overturn any verdict after the roll, death to the Gulag or the Gulag to death, but every overturn puts a chip on him. The Lubyanka has 8 cells; anyone arrested once all 8 are full goes to the troika and is shot without trial.

    The Gulag holds 12 places, two rows of six under the departments, longest held first. The moment the twelfth place fills, an amnesty breaks out: each of the first six goes up into the bottom seat of the department above him, if it is empty, and climbs at once through any empty seats above, with no chips; his owner keeps him. It can happen again whenever the Gulag fills up again. Three Stalin cards, “The Gulag is cold”, kill the two prisoners held longest. When the Red Army's Recruit frees a prisoner, his owner may show his card to keep him; if nobody does (always the case for the old guard), the Red Army player drafts him.

    Exile. Ten men carry a passport, marked on their tiles. As an action, show the card of your passport man and have him flee from any seat, if he carries at least one chip and one of the 3 places abroad is free. The NKVD's Head of Special Tasks (marked with a scope) may send an assassin after an exile at the stage's odds; a miss puts a chip on the NKVD chief. “The long arm” sends Stalin's own assassin after the first man who fled.

    The end. Every hand is counted: any seat 1, exile 1, a cell or the Gulag 0, shot −1. Most points wins; ties go to whoever holds the highest seat.
